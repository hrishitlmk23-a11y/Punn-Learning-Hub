Summarizing & Organizing Ideas

Traditional Japanese Tale

The Stonecutter

Long ago in Japan, a stonecutter grew tired of his hard work and wished to become a rich merchant. A mountain spirit granted his wish, yet the man soon envied a powerful prince, then the blazing sun, then a dark raincloud, and finally the wind. Each time he transformed into something stronger, he discovered something else with even greater power. When he became a giant mountain rock that even the wind could not move, he suddenly heard a steady tap-tap-tap at his feet—a humble stonecutter was chipping away at him. Realizing his own quiet strength, he asked to become himself again.

Model 3-Step Summary:
1. Beginning: A tired Japanese stonecutter wishes to become more powerful.
2. Middle: After transforming into a merchant, prince, sun, cloud, wind, and giant rock, he hears a stonecutter chipping him away.
3. Lesson: True strength comes from appreciating who you already are.

Editor's Desk — Grammar & Clauses

1. Tenses & Pronouns

“Yesterday, the runner breaks the record and forgot their water bottle.”

“Yesterday, the runner broke the record and forgot his/her water bottle.” Rule: Consistent past tense + clear singular pronoun
2. Homophones

“Their sure the secret key is hidden over they're, beside there old map.”

“They're sure the secret key is hidden over there, beside their old map.” Rule: They're = they are • there = place • their = belonging
3. Direct Speech

the coach shouted pass the ball before the whistle blows

The coach shouted, "Pass the ball before the whistle blows!" Rule: Capital letter, comma before speech marks, punctuation inside quotes
4. Sentence Upgrade (Clauses & Adjectives)

Combine into 1 vivid Year 5 sentence: “The match was intense. The team stayed calm. They scored in the final minute.”

“Although the match was fiercely intense, the calm team kept fighting and scored a thrilling goal in the final minute.” Rule: Use a subordinate clause + vivid adjectives/adverbs to link ideas
